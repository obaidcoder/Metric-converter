General Questions

What is the metric system?

The metric system, also known as the International System of Units (SI), is a decimal-based system of measurement used worldwide. It consists of base units like meters (length), kilograms (mass), and seconds (time), along with their decimal multiples and subdivisions.

Why should I use the metric system?

The metric system offers several advantages:

  • Universal acceptance and use in science and international trade
  • Simple decimal-based conversions
  • Consistent relationship between units
  • Standard prefixes across all measurements

Accuracy and Precision

How many decimal places should I use?

The number of decimal places depends on your needs:

  • General use: 2-3 decimal places is usually sufficient
  • Scientific work: 4-6 decimal places may be necessary
  • Engineering: Follow industry standards for your field

What is significant figures and why do they matter?

Significant figures indicate the precision of a measurement. They include all certain digits plus one uncertain digit. Maintaining appropriate significant figures ensures you're not implying more precision than your original measurement had.
